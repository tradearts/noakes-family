  1. c.1818–1903
    Francis Joseph Gough Galway → Melbourne
    Born in the parish of Kilconly, near Tuam, Co. Galway. Catholic, could read and write. With his wife Margaret he sailed on the barque Mary Nixon (left London July 1841, called at Cork in August) and landed at Port Phillip, Melbourne, on 30 Nov 1841. The trip

Following the Noakes line, about 10 days in England

This is the side all three of us share. Mum's-side extras are at the end.

1 · London, 2 days

Four Noakes generations in four City churches, all walkable: St Dunstan-in-the-West (Fleet St), St Sepulchre near Holborn, St Botolph Aldgate and St Helen's Bishopsgate.

2 · Sussex, day trip

Eastbourne, home of Frances Young (George Noakes' wife), and Plumpton near Lewes, where Mary Browne was born in 1719.

3 · Worcestershire, 2 days

Hanley Castle, the oldest Noakes home we've found (1600s), beside Upton-upon-Severn and the Malvern Hills. Check the parish records for this link before we go.

4 · Liverpool, 2 days

Where Thomas Noakes was born in 1862 before emigrating to Sydney. Everton, Walton, Anfield Cemetery (Sarah Ann Noakes' grave) and the Merseyside Maritime Museum's emigration story.

5 · Sunderland, 1 day

Thomas Noakes married Sarah Ann Marns here in 1845. Her family had been in Sunderland at least two generations.

Extra · Mum's side in England

Dorset (Wareham, the Purbeck coast) and the Forest of Dean, 40 minutes from Hanley Castle.

Add-on: the Irish lines, about 6 days

Fly Dublin in, drive west and north, finish at Cork. Mum's side, so this one is mainly for Bryce and his brother.

1 · Dublin, 1 day

Home of the McDonalds and Grays. The National Library of Ireland's Catholic parish registers are free online and the best next step for finding Francis Gough's parents.

2 · Kilconly & Tuam, Co. Galway

The Gough home parish, a small rural parish just north of Tuam. About 2.5 hours from Dublin.

3 · Manorhamilton, Co. Leitrim

Where Sarah Harkins died in 1886: the Kerrigan country. Close to the Leitrim–Cavan area the DNA also points to.

Via · Carlow, Baltinglass & Templemore

On the road west from Dublin: the Lawlers (Carlow), Cunninghams (Baltinglass, Wicklow) and Cardens (Templemore, Tipperary), the families of Lillian Gough's mother.

4 · Co. Clare

Margaret Rickards' home county (Rickards and Moriarty families). The Cliffs of Moher and the Burren are on the way south.

5 · Cork & Cobh

The Mary Nixon called at Cork in August 1841 on its way to Melbourne. The Cobh Heritage Centre tells the emigration story.

How sure are we?

  • From Robert Seavor Noakes back to Thomas Noakes of Liverpool is backed by census, marriage and death records.
  • Most of the older ancestors were matched from other researchers' trees on Ancestry. Each one had supporting records, and the dates and places fitted our family.
  • The weakest point is the move from Worcestershire to London around 1700. Check it with parish records before planning a trip around Hanley Castle.
  • Gough line: Ancestry runs out at Francis Joseph Gough (born c.1818, Kilconly). His parents will be in the Irish Catholic parish registers, which Ancestry indexes but doesn't show on the current membership. The Kerrigan, Harkins, McDonald and Gray parents came from other researchers' trees and are less certain.
  • Not known yet: Susannah Newman's parents, Mary Brace's parents, and the Stafford family and Mum's mother's family. Family members probably know more than the records do.
